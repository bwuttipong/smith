# High Agency

High agency is the ability to act independently and make decisions regardless of external constraints or social pressures. As George Mack writes: **"You are happening to life"** — you don't view the future as static, but as something to be shaped by human action.

## The Tricycle Model

High agency consists of three skills that must work together:

| Skill | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Clear thinking** | Ability to define problems in simple terms, question assumptions |
| **Bias to action** | Moving ideas from theory into the real world without paralysis |
| **Disagreeability** | Willingness to go against social pressure and authority |

Remove one wheel and the whole thing stops.

## The Software

Five mental models that high agency people install:

1. **There's no unsolvable problem** — if it doesn't defy physics, it's solvable
2. **There's no way** — there's no single "right way" to do things, only what works for you
3. **There are no adults** — nobody has it figured out; kill your gurus
4. **There's no normal** — normal is forgotten, only weird survives
5. **There's only now** — the past and future don't exist; you have a finite number of nows

## Low Agency Traps

| Trap | Root Cause | Escape Route |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| Vague Trap | Never defining the problem | Write it down, draw it, talk it out |
| Midwit Trap | Overcomplicating simple actions | Inversion: what would the left-brain do? |
| Attachment Trap | Too attached to past assumptions | "What would I do with 10x agency?" |
| Rumination Trap | Stuck in "what if it goes wrong?" loops | Reframe decisions as experiments |
| Overwhelm Trap | Daunting task = paralysis | Chunk into video-game levels |

## Key Insight

Optimism = glass half full. Pessimism = glass half empty. **High agency = you're a tap.** You look in the mirror and see a giant tap staring back at you.
