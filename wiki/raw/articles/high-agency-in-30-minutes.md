# High Agency in 30 Minutes

*Written by George Mack*

**Warning: This is not doom scrolling.** It's an idea so simple yet effective it may change how you view reality.

It takes 30 minutes on average to read.

## What is high agency?

I believe high agency might be the most important idea of the 21st century. When I first learned of high agency, it felt like a secret that had been hidden from me.

So, what is it?

In a 1964 public inquiry into whether the film *The Lovers* should be banned for its adult content, Supreme Court judge Justice Potter Stewart was asked to define pornography. He replied with the ultimate non-definition:

**"I know it when I see it".**

High agency is one of those "I know it when I see it" ideas. And once you see it – you can't unsee it.

### High Agency in a Question

You wake up in a 3rd world jail cell. You're only allowed to call one person you know to get you out of there. **Who do you call?**

Why did you choose this person? What is it about them that made you pick up the phone?

**This person you picked has something.** A spark. A *je ne sais quoi*. That 'something' is high agency.

### How To Spot High Agency People

1. **Weird teenage hobbies** — Teenage years are the hardest time to go against social pressures. If they can go against the crowd as a teenager, they can go against the crowd as an adult.
2. **Treadmill energy** — If you meet with them when you're tired and defeated, you leave the room ready to run a marathon.
3. **You can never guess their opinion** — The boxer who writes poetry. The advertiser obsessed with the history of war. The beauty queen who reads Nietzsche.
4. **Immigrant mentality** — If they've moved from their hometown, that's a good sign. Even better if they've moved from their home country.
5. **Sends you niche content** — They look at the content itself, not the social engagement around it.
6. **Mean to your face, nice behind your back** — Swimming against the social tide requires agency.
7. **Quit something of prestige** — The miserable management consultant who breaks free from golden handcuffs to become a stand-up comedian.
8. **They don't trust. They verify** — "They say" → Who is they? "Science says" → What is the science?
9. **Self-taught learning machines** — Tesla, Da Vinci and Darwin didn't ask for permission.
10. **They question the question** — The right answer to the wrong question is worse than no answer to the right question.

### The High Agency Spectrum

The person you'd call from a 3rd world jail cell represents the most high agency person you know. If you created a room with everyone you'd call in that scenario, what would they have in common?

It's not age, gender, race, education, job titles or politics. It's not optimism or pessimism either.

Optimism states the glass is half full. Pessimism states the glass is half empty.

**High agency states you're a tap.** You look in the mirror and see a giant tap staring back at you.

The one big thing everyone in that room has in common: **They are happening to life.** They don't view the future as a static entity. They view it as something to be shaped by human action.

High agency is like a tricycle with three wheels:
1. **Clear thinking**
2. **Bias to action**
3. **Disagreeability**

Remove one wheel and it stops working.

## Part 1 — High Agency Software

Five lines of code high agency people have installed in their heads:

### 1. There's no unsolvable problem

The human brain is a question-answering machine. When faced with a problem, ask: **"Does this defy the laws of physics?"** If no, it's solvable — regardless of what other people say.

In 1961, Claude Shannon and Ed Thorp decided hacking roulette didn't defy the laws of physics. They built the first-ever wearable computer to predict roulette outcomes, improving their odds by 44%.

### 2. There's no way

There was no singular "way" of doing things for Nadal, Djokovic, and Federer — they all found what worked for them. Leonard Cohen took years to write "Hallelujah"; Bob Dylan wrote "Just Like a Woman" in 15 minutes.

### 3. There are no adults

If you dig deep enough under low agency thinking, you discover a belief in a god-like adult class. A Peter Pan perception of reality. The fictional beliefs of Santa were debugged — but a belief in perfect adults who run the world still remains.

Even the greats were deeply human:
- Steve Jobs delayed cancer treatment for a carrot juice diet
- Isaac Newton spent 30 years writing 1M words on alchemy
- Napoleon's haemorrhoids may have cost him Waterloo
- MLK had extra-marital affairs
- Dostoevsky gambled away everything

Don't put any adult on a pedestal. Kill your gurus.

### 4. There's no normal

The normal paradox: We hide our weirdness to be liked — but the tribe forgets normal behaviour. **Normal is forgotten. Only weird survives.**

### 5. There's only now

The past no longer exists. The future does not yet exist. Life is just a series of nows. You have a finite number of them.

## Part 2 — The Highest Agency Human Ever?

Meet **Wilbur Wright**.

It's 1885. He's 18, had a fantastic childhood, bright future at Yale. Then a local psychopath (on cocaine prescription — common at the time) smashes his face in with a hockey stick.

Wilbur spent years bedbound. Yale dreams cancelled. His mother terminally ill.

But Wilbur was obsessed with a question: **If birds can fly, why can't humans?**

From his sickbed, he wrote letters to libraries across the country requesting every book on birds, physics, and mechanics. He concluded:
- Birds can fly
- So flying doesn't defy the laws of physics
- So humans can fly
- So **Wilbur can fly**

He and his brother Orville faced endless "impossible" problems:
- **Wrong location** → Contacted Weather Bureau, found Kitty Hawk had the best winds
- **700 miles away** → Packed up and took a train (they'd never left their hometown)
- **Controlling flight** → Stole the warped-wing system from birds, tested 200 wing shapes in a homemade wind tunnel
- **Engines too heavy** → Calculated specs and built a custom 180lb aluminium engine

On December 17, 1903 — Wilbur and Orville were the first non-birds ever to fly. Wilbur's flight: 59 seconds, 582 feet.

Last year, 5.58 billion passenger seats were flown. It all started from "two nuts" pretending to be birds on a beach. **Wilbur Wright might be the highest agency human ever to live.**

## Part 3 — Escaping Low Agency Traps

### 1. The Vague Trap
Hides from agency by never defining the problem. The average person has 10-60,000 thoughts per day — most aren't even clear sentences.

**Escape route:** Define the problem in simple words out of your head. Write it down. Draw it. Use a whiteboard. The vague trap is often downstream from vague questions.

### 2. The Midwit Trap
Overcomplicates agency. The guy on the left isn't smart enough to overcomplicate simple actions. The guy on the right is smart enough to reduce complexity. The midwit in the middle mistakes simplicity for stupidity.

**Escape route:** What would the guy on the left do? Use inversion — flip the problem around. E.g., "How would I become a worse writer?" → Don't write. Write inconsistently. Write boring stuff. Flip: Write. Write consistently. Write what excites you.

### 3. The Attachment Trap
Too attached to past assumptions. The three most powerful mind-altering drugs: DMT, meth, and momentum.

**Escape route:** What would I do if I had 10x the agency? This question throws a first-principles grenade into your mind.

### 4. The Rumination Trap
Being stuck in never-ending "what if it goes wrong?" loops. The ruminating perfectionist keeps kicking cans down the road.

**Escape route:** How can I take action on this now? Reframe decisions as experiments. You're not a perfectionist frozen on stage — you're a curious scientist testing a hypothesis.

### 5. The Overwhelm Trap
The task is so daunting you don't know where to begin. You look at where you are (level 0) vs the ideal (level 100) and run away.

**Escape route:** What's the smallest first step? Think like a video game — chunk everything into levels small enough to create momentum.

## Part 4 — Turning Bullshit Into Reality

### The Exercise

1. Open a blank page. Write down esoteric things you value (e.g. "High Agency")
2. Write down 10 specific ways to display that value with action
3. Pick the one that gives you the strongest sense of fear
4. Write down every micro step
5. Do each micro step now
6. Feel the emotion of being a live player
7. Come back tomorrow and repeat

### Tools

1. **The High Agency Flow Chart** — A simple flow chart to escape low agency traps
2. **The Swedish House Mafia Technique** — Collect the smartest people you know, lock the door, rally ideas like a tennis game
3. **The Story Razor** — Between two options, ask: "What is the best story?" *Do it for the plot.*
4. **Ask For Help** — Write a good description of your problem. Try to predict what they'll say. Realize you often don't need to ask.
5. **A Change of Perspective is Worth 50 IQ Points** — The wealthy man parking his Rolls Royce for $15.41.
