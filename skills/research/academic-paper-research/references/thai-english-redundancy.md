# Thai-English Redundancy: L1 Transfer Patterns

> Session date: 2026-06-26
> Research for Jeff (Thai native speaker) about why he speaks redundantly in English

## Core Finding

Thai speakers' redundancy in English is **systematic L1 transfer**, not errors. Thai uses word repetition (reduplication) as a normal grammatical feature for emphasis, intensity, and clarity — this carries directly into English production.

## Concrete Redundancy Examples with Explanations

### 1. Adjective Repetition for Emphasis ("chill chill", "many many", "big big")

| Example | Context |
|---------|---------|
| "Let's have a walk **chill chill**" | Thai calque — "chill chill" means "casually / relaxed" |
| "I started to dream about walking in the street with **many, many** buildings on both sides" | Chutisilp (1984) literary example |
| "seeing myself in a **big, big** school" | Chutisilp (1984) — reduplication for emphasis |

**Mechanism:** Thai natively uses adjective/verb repetition as a grammatical intensifier (e.g., เร็วๆ *reo-reo* = "quick quick" = "quickly"). This transfers directly.

### 2. Grade/School Level Double-Marking

| Example | Source |
|---------|--------|
| "Since the **fourth grade in an elementary school**..." | Singhasak & Methitham (2016), Participant P4 |
| "In the **tenth to twelfth grade of secondary school**..." | Singhasak & Methitham (2016), Participant P4 |

**Mechanism:** Thai education system divides school levels differently (Pratomsuksa 1-6, Mathayomsuksa 1-6) vs. Western K-12. Speakers redundantly specify both the grade number AND the school tier to ensure the reader understands.

### 3. Verb Repetition for Continuous Action

| Example | Source |
|---------|--------|
| "**ate and ate and ate** but he could not eat all the rice" | Chutisilp (1984) — Thai literary style in English |

**Mechanism:** Thai uses verb serialization/repetition to express ongoing action. English typically uses adverbs or tense for this.

### 4. Redundant Pronoun/Noun Phrases

| Example | Source |
|---------|--------|
| "Like **my one friend**, he told me..." | Singhasak & Methitham (2016), Participant P6 — direct translation from Thai |
| "I also got **unimpressive scores and unimpressive commends**" | Singhasak & Methitham (2016), Participant P3 — repetition for emphasis |

### 6. Nativized Compounds

| Example | Meaning | Source |
|---------|---------|--------|

| Thai Original | English Produced | Standard English | Source |
|:-------------|:----------------|:----------------|:-------|

### 8. Formulaic Rigidity ("I'm fine, thank you, and you?")

Thai students are taught fixed conversational scripts by rote memorization. Even when sick, they reply "I'm fine, thank you, and you?" — a reflection of the Thai educational system's emphasis on repetition over creative language use. (Singhasak & Methitham, 2016)

## Six Processes of "Thainess" in English (Watkhaolarm, 2005)

| Process | Description | Example |
|---------|-------------|---------|
| Transfer | Cultural/social elements from Thai | Adding kinship terms before names ("Mae Hiang") |
| Translation | Word-for-word English translation | "face them with brave" from Thai |
| Shift | Thai proverbs/idioms shifted to English | |
| Hybridization | Thai+English lexical combinations | "black kawao" (bird species) |
| Lexical borrowing | Thai words when no English equivalent | |
| **Reduplication** | **Syntactic repetition for emphasis** | **"many, many buildings"; "ate and ate and ate"** |

## Underlying Causes

| Cause | Explanation |
|-------|-------------|
| **L1 reduplication norm** | Thai uses word repetition as native grammar feature — transfers directly |
| **Double-thinking process** | Learners translate Thai→English before speaking, producing calqued structures |
| **Rote learning culture** | Thai English education emphasizes memorization of fixed patterns |
| **Grammatical asymmetry** | Thai has no articles/plural marking/tense inflection → omission OR overcorrection |
| **Rhetorical style** | Thai discourse uses repetition for clarity and emphasis |
